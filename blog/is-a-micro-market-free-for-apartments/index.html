Is a Micro Market Really Free for an Apartment Community?

SBy Sam Johnsen, founder, Cache Markets · Published October 16, 2026

TL;DR Yes. With Cache Markets, your property pays nothing for equipment, install, product, stocking, or service. Residents pay for what they buy. Your community provides a small, visible spot, a standard outlet, and Wi-Fi. There is no revenue share by default, and terms like notice period and install timing are worked out with each property, not forced on you.

Short answer: yes, and here is why

Property managers from Sandy to Lehi ask us the same first question: "What does this cost us?" The honest answer is $0. Cache Markets owns the micro market. We buy and install the fridge, shelving, and tap-to-pay kiosk, we stock it, and we restock and service it. Residents pay at the kiosk with a card or phone, and that is how we get paid.

The model only works if the market sells, so our incentive is the same as yours: put it where residents actually walk, keep it full, and keep it clean.

What does the property actually give up?

Space

A Cache market can fit in a footprint as small as 30 x 30 inches. Common spots are a clubhouse corner, a lobby wall, a mail or package room, or an amenity floor. The spots that tend to make the most sense are the ones residents pass every day, like a mail room wall or a clubhouse kitchen counter.

Power and Wi-Fi

The market plugs into a standard outlet, and the kiosk connects over the property's Wi-Fi.

Staff time

None for restocking. Your office team does not handle cash, inventory, or refunds. If something breaks, the office just lets us know.

Is there a catch? Contracts, insurance, and cancelling

We start with a short, one-page letter of intent (LOI) so everyone agrees on the spot and the basics before anything is installed. A few points property managers usually care about:

  • Insurance: we provide a certificate of insurance (COI) for your location before install.
  • Notice to cancel and install timing: agreed with each property and written into the LOI. There is no one-size-fits-all default.
  • Revenue share: none by default. The market is a free resident amenity.
  • Equipment: stays Cache's. If the market ever leaves, we remove it.

Micro market vs vending machine for residents

A vending machine is a coil-and-glass box with a short list of chips and soda. A micro market is an open, self-serve shop: residents grab fresh food, drinks, snacks, and essentials and pay at the kiosk. That means more variety and a better amenity to show on a tour. The honest tradeoff: a micro market needs a visible spot, like a clubhouse or lobby, while a vending machine can sit almost anywhere. See our micro market vs vending machine comparison.

Do renters want it?

According to the Greystar 2024 Design Survey, which gathered responses from 90,368 renters, 47% of renters are interested in a convenience shop for small purchases, and renters indicated a $53 per month premium for it. For a community competing on amenities, a 24/7 shop in the building is a visible, everyday perk that costs the property nothing to add. See the numbers on our homepage.

What a free walkthrough looks like in Sandy, Lehi, or Draper

Our site assessment is a short visit to your property:

  1. We look at two or three candidate spots with you.
  2. We check the outlet, Wi-Fi signal, and foot traffic from the leasing office and mail area.
  3. We follow up with a mockup of the market in your chosen spot and a one-page LOI to review with your regional manager or owner.

There is no obligation. If your space is not a fit, we will tell you. We serve apartment communities from Provo to North Salt Lake, including Sandy, Draper, Lehi, and the rest of Utah County.

FAQ

How much does a micro market cost the property?

Nothing. Cache Markets pays for the equipment, install, product, and service.

Does the property earn anything?

There is no revenue share by default. The benefit to the property is a free, 24/7 resident amenity.

How long does install take?

Install timing is agreed with each property in the letter of intent, and we confirm the date with you before anything is installed.

What if something goes missing?

The equipment and inventory belong to Cache Markets. Your office team does not handle cash, inventory, or refunds.

Can we cancel?

Yes. The notice period is agreed per property in the letter of intent, and Cache removes the equipment.

Who signs off on it?

Usually the community manager and their regional manager or the owner. We are happy to bring the mockup and LOI to whoever makes the call.
